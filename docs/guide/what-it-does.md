# What ChamBoard does

When you type into an app or website, you give away more than the words you send. ChamBoard is a keyboard for Android phones that closes three of those gaps.

## 1. Your unsent drafts

**The problem.** Many websites watch the text box *while* you type. They can see sentences you delete, searches you never run, and messages you rewrite before sending. Search bars are the worst offenders: every letter you type is sent off to suggest completions.

**What ChamBoard does.** You type into a private notepad built into the keyboard. The website sees nothing until you press **Emit**. Then only the finished text arrives.

*Everyday comparison:* writing a letter at home and posting only the final copy, instead of writing it at the post office counter while the clerk reads over your shoulder.

## 2. Your typing rhythm

**The problem.** Everyone types with a personal rhythm: how fast you are, which letter pairs you rush, where you hesitate. Fraud-detection and tracking companies measure this rhythm. It can recognise you across different accounts, the way a voice can be recognised on the phone.

**What ChamBoard does.** When you press Emit, the keyboard types your text *for* you, at a made-up but realistic human pace. Each mask comes with its own made-up typist: their speed, their pauses, the letter pairs they rush, and even the occasional typo they make and fix. None of it comes from how you actually type, so the rhythm the website records isn't yours. And because the typist stays the same per app, your bank doesn't see a "different person" every time you log in.

*Everyday comparison:* having a stranger read your letter aloud, so listeners hear the words but not your voice.

## 3. Your writing style

**The problem.** Everyone also has writing habits: favourite punctuation, particular misspellings, British or American spelling, "don't" versus "do not". Computers can compare these habits across posts and guess that two anonymous accounts belong to the same person.

**What ChamBoard does.** Before sending, it rewrites your habits into those of a **mask**: a made-up but realistic writer with habits of their own. One mask might use British spelling, say "lol" and skip the final full stop; another might write "haha", use dashes and always capitalise. Your own habits are replaced, not just hidden. You always see the rewritten version next to your original before anything is sent.

Each app gets its own mask automatically, and it stays the same over time, because a real person writes the same way on the same account. You can also create named masks (say, one for a forum account), pin them to apps, and swap a mask for a fresh one whenever you like. Two of your accounts end up looking like two different ordinary people, and neither looks like "someone using a privacy keyboard".

*Everyday comparison:* wearing a different, convincing costume for each place you visit, and the same one every time you go back.

If lots of people use ChamBoard some day, there's also a **Uniform** option where everyone shares one plain style, like a crowd in identical raincoats.

## Built to be trustworthy

- **It can't go online.** The app doesn't ask for internet access, so it can't send your typing anywhere. The build is checked automatically so this can't change by accident.
- **It forgets.** Drafts are erased when you switch text boxes, turn off your screen, or leave the keyboard closed for more than a minute (you choose how long, including "immediately"). Nothing you type is saved.
- **It has its own clipboard.** Copying inside ChamBoard goes to a private clipboard that other apps can't see and that empties itself after a while. Pasting from your phone's normal clipboard, or copying out to it, only happens when you tap the button for it. Anything copied out is hidden from clipboard history and cleared after a minute.
- **Convenience is your call.** Anything that trades a little privacy for less hassle, like the one-minute draft grace, the paste button, "Enter sends", and word suggestions, is a switch in settings with a one-line explanation.
- **It's open source.** Anyone can read exactly what it does.
